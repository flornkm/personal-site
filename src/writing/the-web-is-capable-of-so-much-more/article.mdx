# The web is capable of so much more

A couple of days ago I started an experiment based on a simple question:

_What's the furthest you can go building a web-based experience?_

In recent weeks, the amount of experimental (and what I would consider "crazy") stuff being shipped seemed to have reached an all-time high.

## The idea

There were a few things I knew right away from an art-direction standpoint:

- The experience should feel satisfying, like a game
- Nobody should have to enter account details or log in, just open the website and you're ready
- The experience should be entirely web-based and coded
- Execution should rely heavily on AI so we can move fast
- It must run on every device I could think of

### Figuring things out

After a lot of back and forth on the _what_, I came to the conclusion that an open-world experience in WebGL would likely be the best option. I've seen countless ThreeJS-based games, so I collected some of them:

On top of that, I really like dither effects. And since this had to be extremely performant, why not use dithering not just as a style, but to actually lower the resolution of the experience, so clients have to render far fewer pixels?

After all the preparation, I started writing a super long prompt, including the plan I had discussed with my agent beforehand and some decisions on styling, capabilities, etc.

My model of choice, which I used all along, was **Opus 5.5 on Ultracode**.

My agent spawned countless subagents and ran for well over `14 hours` without a single pause.

## The result

After a few hours of watching my agent figure things out, I couldn't wait to test what he had built. The AI went with the following tech stack:

- WebGL 2.0 (instead of WebGPU, so even older browsers are supported)
- A custom engine instead of ThreeJS
- Sounds, models and assets, all as `.ts` files
- `socket.io` for full multiplayer support, including server-side entities

## Does this mean you should go crazy?

No, of course not. Less is often more. Look at my site - I could've turned everything into a 3D-ish experience with sound effects and whatnot. But I didn't. I think there's a lot of beauty in an insanely simple interface that hides a lot of complexity and functionality behind simple control layers. The best examples are many of today's AI-native products - on-demand UI is having its moment, and it'll only get more important from here.

That said, I believe using AI simply to be creative and test the boundaries of the modern web helps us think outside the box, and ultimately leads to more variety in the websites and platforms we use every day.
